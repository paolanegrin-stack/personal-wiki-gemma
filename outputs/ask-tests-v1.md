## 1. What should a project charter include?

### Answer

The project charter should include:

*   Project title, brief description, reason for doing it, and link to strategy [1].
*   Specific objectives, expected benefits, results, and major deliverables [1].
*   Assumptions that must later be checked, high-level scope and boundaries [1].
*   Major milestones and timeline, including effects on existing systems or teams [1].
*   Roles and contact details, including initial funding, people, and other resources [1].
*   Main risks and responses, success measures and targets, and approval signatures [1].

## 2. How can a company decide which projects to fund when it has more ideas than money?

### Answer

Selection means choosing a set of projects that supports the organization's goals while considering each project's benefits, costs, risks, and resource needs [2]. Managers should combine project categories, financial measures, and nonfinancial criteria [2].

A practical portfolio process involves:
1. Forming a project council and identifying relevant stakeholders [1].
2. Setting project categories and selection criteria [1].
3. Collecting comparable information for each proposal and checking available resources [1].
4. Removing proposals or criteria that do not help the decision [1].
5. Ranking projects within categories, selecting those to fund, and keeping others in reserve [1].
6. Putting the decision process into practice and reviewing it as circumstances change [1].

Leaders can use five early yes/no screens: Is the project mandatory for an external reason? Does it meet the minimum IRR? Is its payback within the company's limit? Is the risk acceptable? Does it fit current corporate strategy? These screens can narrow the list before detailed comparisons [1].

Leaders can also rank projects by fastest payback, highest NPV, or highest IRR, and add strategy, implementation difficulty, risk, project dependencies, and synergies to produce another answer [4]. Leaders from finance, distribution, production, sales, marketing, and strategy may value projects differently [4]. The lesson is to make criteria and trade-offs visible, check conflicts with corporate strategy, and consider how projects work together [4].
