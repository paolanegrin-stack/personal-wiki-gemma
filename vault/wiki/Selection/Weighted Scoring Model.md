Selection methods include nonnumeric approaches like executive preference and checklists, as well as financial methods such as payback period and net present value. A weighted scoring model is a nonfinancial method where importance is assigned to criteria to score proposals, calculated as the sum of criterion scores multiplied by their weights.

## Key points

- Nonnumeric selection methods include an executive's favored project, an operating or competitive necessity, and a checklist comparing project attributes. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- The payback period calculates the initial investment divided by annual net cash inflow when inflows are roughly constant, indicating how quickly the initial investment is recovered. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- Net present value (NPV) discounts future net cash flows to today's value and subtracts the investment; a positive NPV suggests the project meets the required return. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- A weighted scoring model assigns importance to each criterion and scores proposals by calculating the total as the sum of (criterion score times criterion weight). (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
