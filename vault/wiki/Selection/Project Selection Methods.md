Organizations face the challenge of selecting projects that align with their goals by considering benefits, costs, risks, and resource needs. Selection involves combining project categories, financial measures, and nonfinancial criteria. A practical process involves forming a council, setting criteria, collecting information, ranking projects, and using early yes/no screens to choose a balanced set of fundable and deliverable projects.

## Key points

- Selection requires choosing projects that support organizational goals by considering each project's benefits, costs, risks, and resource needs. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 1)
- Projects can be classified as compliance, strategic, or operational, and leaders decide how to allocate funding across categories to prevent portfolio imbalance. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 2)
- Financial methods include the payback period, which calculates the time to recover initial investment, and Net Present Value (NPV), which discounts future net cash flows to today's value and subtracts the investment. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- Nonfinancial methods can use a weighted scoring model to assign importance to criteria, such as strategy fit or ease of implementation, to score proposals. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- A practical portfolio process involves forming a project council, setting criteria, collecting information, ranking projects, and using early yes/no screens to narrow the list. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 5)
