Financial evaluation methods include the payback period, which measures the time to recover initial investment, and the net present value (NPV), which discounts future net cash flows to today's value and subtracts the investment. Nonfinancial methods involve capturing factors like strategic fit and technical feasibility, and can use a weighted scoring model to assign importance to different criteria.

## Key points

- Nonnumeric selection methods include an executive's favored project, an operating or competitive necessity, and a checklist comparing project attributes. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- The payback period calculates the initial investment divided by annual net cash inflow when inflows are roughly constant, indicating how quickly the initial investment is recovered. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- Net present value (NPV) discounts future net cash flows to today's value and subtracts the investment; a positive NPV suggests the project meets the required return. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- Nonfinancial methods capture factors such as fit with the mission, core skills, urgency, strategic significance, market position, brand, technical feasibility, commercial success, and ability to build valuable capabilities. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
- A weighted scoring model assigns importance to each criterion, where the total for a project is the sum of (criterion score times criterion weight). (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 3)
