Project success is determined by performance or scope, cost, and time, which are interconnected factors. Beyond meeting the original schedule and budget, teams should also measure outcomes such as benefits, value created, impact, opportunities, and risks. Success involves deciding on acceptable trade-offs between these elements.

## Key points

- The direct goals of a project are performance or scope, cost, and time, and these factors are interdependent. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 3)
- Teams should measure outcomes including benefits, value created, impact, opportunities, and risks, alongside delivery date and cost, which are part of the result. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 3)
- Projects balance scope or performance, time, cost, and quality, and priorities differ based on factors like a fixed event date or a product launch. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 4)
- A priority matrix helps managers decide which goal to constrain, enhance, or accept when requirements change or work needs acceleration. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 4)
