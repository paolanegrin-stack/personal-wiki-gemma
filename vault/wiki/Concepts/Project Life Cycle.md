The project life cycle consists of four broad stages: Initiation, Planning, Execution and control, and Termination. Planning is a critical stage where work is broken down, roles are assigned, budgets are estimated, schedules are developed, and resources are allocated. The course emphasizes planning, covering various settings like IT, construction, and product development, and mentions standards and organizations such as the Project Management Institute (PMI) and PRINCE2.

## Key points

- The project life cycle follows four broad stages: Initiation, Planning, Execution and control, and Termination. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 4)
- The Planning stage involves breaking work into parts using a work breakdown structure (WBS), assigning roles with a responsibility or RACI matrix, estimating the budget, developing the schedule, and allocating resources. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 4)
- Scheduling tools mentioned include Gantt charts, PERT, CPM, and methods for dealing with limited resources. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 4)
- The Initiation stage requires explaining the need, selecting and prioritizing projects, identifying stakeholders, and establishing a project charter and statement of work. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 4)
