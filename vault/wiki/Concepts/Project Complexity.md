Project complexity has structural, sociopolitical, and emergent forms. These cover size, teams, tasks, and dependencies; stakeholder interests, organizational relationships, and conflict; and new technology, changing methods, and uncertainty, respectively. Mapping these forms helps teams plan resources, anticipate risks, and choose a management approach.

## Key points

- A responsibility matrix lists activities against people involved, clarifying who does the work, who supports it, where teams must coordinate, and what authority each person has. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 4)
- Projects balance scope or performance, time, cost, and quality, and priorities differ based on factors like a fixed event date or a product launch. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 4)
- A priority matrix helps determine which goal to constrain (keep fixed), enhance (improve if possible), or accept (allow some flexibility) when requirements change or work needs acceleration. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 4)
- Complexity can be mapped in three forms: structural (size, team, tasks, and dependencies), sociopolitical (stakeholder interests, organizational relationships, and conflict), and emergent (new technology, changing methods, and uncertainty). (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 4)
