Organizations utilize projects to implement changes and execute strategy, which can lead to a competitive advantage. Projects deliver unique results, while programs group related projects sharing goals or timelines, and portfolios group projects, programs, and sometimes operations to achieve strategic goals by selecting priorities and allocating resources toward the most valuable work.

## Key points

- Organizations use projects to make changes and carry out strategy, and a well-run project can create a competitive advantage. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 5)
- A project delivers one unique result. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 5)
- A program groups related projects that share a goal, dependencies, or a common timeline and budget, and managing them together can produce benefits that the individual projects could not deliver alone. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 5)
- A portfolio groups projects, programs, and sometimes operations to achieve strategic goals, and portfolio management selects priorities and moves resources toward the work that adds the most value. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 5)
