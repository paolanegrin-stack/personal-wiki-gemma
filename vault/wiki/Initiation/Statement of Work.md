The Statement of Work (SOW), called the project scope statement in these slides, refines the high-level scope from the project charter into a detailed description. It specifies what the team will deliver and do, what will be excluded, fixed dates, and supporting assumptions. An "is / is not" list helps define boundaries clearly to prevent scope creep.

## Key points

- The SOW, or project scope statement, develops the charter's high-level scope into a more detailed description. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 3)
- The SOW details what the team will deliver and do, what it will exclude, which dates cannot move, and which assumptions support the estimates. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 3)
- A useful scope checklist can cover the objective, deliverables, milestones, technical requirements, acceptance criteria, limits, exclusions, and customer review. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 3)
- An "is / is not" list makes ambiguous boundaries concrete to protect against scope creep. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 3)
