Stakeholder management is a continuous process involving identifying, prioritizing, and engaging with people who are affected by or can affect the project. Stakeholder satisfaction is one measure of success; teams should understand stakeholder needs and match communication levels to their impact. Key actions include informing, consulting, involving, collaborating, and empowering stakeholders.

## Key points

- Stakeholder management is continuous and involves three repeating steps: Identify, Prioritize, and Understand and engage. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 5)
- Stakeholders should be identified by listing people and groups with an interest, influence, or exposure to the outcome, including internal examples like the sponsor and external examples like customers and regulators. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 5)
- Stakeholders should be prioritized by mapping them by influence or power and by interest, paying particular attention to those with high influence and high interest. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 5)
- Engagement promises include Informing (providing updates), Consulting (adding listening and feedback), Involving (working together so concerns shape options), Collaborating (giving advice and recommendations a stronger role), and Empowering (implementing the decision made by the stakeholder group). (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 5)
- A practical portfolio process involves forming a project council, setting selection criteria, collecting comparable information, ranking projects, and using early yes/no screens to narrow the list. (source: 02_Project_Selection_Methods_Summary.md, section 5)
