The project charter formally authorizes a project and its manager, providing high-level requirements and a basis for resource commitment. It serves as a shared target, outlining the project's title, objectives, expected benefits, major deliverables, assumptions, milestones, risks, and success measures. The planning sequence begins with the project charter and statement of work (SOW).

## Key points

- The project charter is issued or approved by the sponsor and formally authorizes the project and its manager, providing high-level requirements and a basis to commit resources. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 2)
- The charter should include the project title, brief description, reason for doing it, link to strategy, specific objectives, expected benefits, results, and major deliverables. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 2)
- Objectives should be SMART, meaning specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound, stating the result and how success will be tested. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 2)
- The planning sequence moves from a high-level project charter and statement of work (SOW) to deliverables, a work breakdown structure (WBS), task sequence and estimates, resource allocation, risks, and a detailed plan. (source: 03_Project_Initiation_and_Stakeholders_Summary.md, section 1)
- The initiation stage of the project life cycle involves explaining the need, selecting and prioritizing projects, identifying stakeholders, and establishing a project charter and statement of work. (source: 01_Project_Management_Introduction_Summary.md, section 4)
